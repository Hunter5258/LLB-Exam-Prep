## C.2 The CrPC versus BNSS ambiguity, stated explicitly

Your prompt states that "the newer syllabus prescribes the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023", while the supplied syllabus prints CrPC sections, and you directed this report to follow the supplied syllabus. The honest position:

1. If the 2025 examination is in fact notified under BNSS, every Part D prediction remains examinable; the cited provisions would change in number and, in places, wording (FIR-to-arrest renumbering, "proclamation and attachment" language, the victim-compensation position, plus new statutory machinery such as designated-officer and forensic provisions that have no historical question lineage).
2. No BNSS section number is asserted anywhere in this report, because verification against an authoritative text was out of scope for this run (your instruction). If you confirm BNSS governs, rework this subsection with a verified CrPC-to-BNSS table before quoting any number in an answer.
3. The predictions assume the supplied CrPC-numbered syllabus. An exact-repeat candidate (Type [ER]) is phrased as the historical wording, which is safe under either regime at topic level.
4. What does not carry over safely under a BNSS regime: chapter and section-number fill-ins (A.3) would need full renumbering, and changed-terminology objective items (for example S. 162 protection, now a different provision) would be reworded. The long-answer predictions (Part D) largely survive.

# Part F: Priority revision list

## F.1 Core provisions to memorise (ranked by combined evidence)

1. **FIR**: S. 154 (recording, entitlement, non-substantive evidence), Ss. 156–157, S. 162, plus the second-FIR jurisprudence (the 2020 problem question; the Ram Lal Narang line of cases, if your notes cover it).

3. **Complaint and cognizance**: definition in S. 2(d); S. 200 (examination, postponement, decision on process), Ss. 201–204 (transfer, postponement with local-inquiry discretion, dismissal, issue of process), S. 210, and the taking-cognizance doctrine (2020 Q5(i)).

11. **Removal of nuisance and S. 145**: S. 133 (conditional order), S. 134 (execution), S. 144A (review of S. 144-type orders); S. 145 (disputing possession, under the status-as-of-date rule), S. 146 (attachment; appointment of receiver), S. 147.
