# 2. Executive Summary

1. **Ten** verified papers are available; 2022 is absent and excluded from every calculation. All recurrence figures in this report therefore use **n = 10**, not 10 years of assumed data.
2. **Nine of ten papers follow one stable architecture:** Q1 = 1×10 objective items (10 marks), Q2 = 2×5 short notes/answers (10 marks), Q3–Q7 = 12 marks each with one internal `Or` alternative (60 marks), total 80, three hours. The 2020 paper (held in 2021) is a 56-mark / 2½-hour Group-A/Group-B variant and is used for topic frequency but not for structure prediction.
3. **The strongest recurring long-answer families**, with verified citations, are:
   - *Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa* / definition of "industry" under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — main slot in 2015 Q4, 2020 Q4, 2021 Q4, 2023 Q4, 2024 Q4, 2025 Q4, plus objectives in 2017 Q1(g) and 2025 Q1(j).

   - Minimum Wages Act, 1948: objects, salient features, fixation/revision — 2015 Q7(Or), 2016 Q6(Or), 2018 Q6(Or), 2019 Q7, 2020 Q6(Or), 2021 Q5, 2024 Q6, 2025 Q6.
   - Factories Act, 1948: health, cleanliness, safety, welfare — 2015 Q5, 2016 Q5, 2018 Q5, 2019 Q6, 2021 Q7(Or), 2023 Q6, 2024 Q5, 2025 Q5.
   - Employees' Compensation / Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923: disabilities, defences, notional extension, dependants, compensation procedure — 2015 Q7, 2016 Q6, 2018 Q6, 2019 Q7, 2020 Q6, 2021 Q6, 2023 Q7, 2024 Q6(Or), 2025 Q6(Or).
   - Assam Shops and Establishments Act — 2015 Q6(Or), 2016 Q7, 2017 Q7, 2018 Q7, 2023 Q7(Or), 2024 Q7, 2025 Q7, plus recurring objectives.
4. **Objective items are highly recycled.** The same factual tests recur for years: unfair labour practice schedule (2015 Q1(a), 2016 Q1(d), 2019 Q1(h), 2020 Q1(d), 2023 Q1(c), 2024 Q1(b)); appropriate Government's referential power (2016 Q1(g), 2018 Q1(g), 2019 Q1(e), 2021 Q1(g), 2023 Q1(a)); conciliation's third neutral party (2019 Q1(b), 2021 Q1(a), 2023 Q1(e), 2024 Q1(f)); and disablement threshold (2015 Q1(c), 2018 Q1(f), 2021 Q1(f)).
5. **The paper is topic-stable and slot-stable.** Since 2015 the same five syllabus blocks have occupied Q3–Q7 in roughly the same order; 2024 and 2025 show a fresh "industrial award and settlement" variant in the Q4 `Or` slot, so rephrasing — not wholesale change — is the normal examiner behaviour.
6. **Coverage gaps exist but are not predictions of absence.** Several official topics (e.g. U2-T03 reference machinery, U4-T02 Commissioner procedure, U1-T05 penalties) appear mainly inside compound questions or objectives; they remain in the complete revision matrix with independent priority.
7. Prediction confidence and revision priority are reported **separately**. A topic may carry high revision priority because it is syllabus-central even where historical evidence is thin. No percentages and no statistical model are claimed.

## 6.4 Long-answer habits

- Marks are frequently split (`4+8`, `6+6`, `8+4`, `2+5+5`, `4+4+4`), signalling that examiners expect a definition or principle first, then the statutory scheme, then analysis or application.
- One `Or` per long slot, normally from the **same unit but a different subtopic** (e.g. 2025 Q4: *Bangalore Water Supply* versus industrial award and settlement; 2024 Q6: Minimum Wages versus compensation liability).
- Problem-based questions are rare but real: 2017 Q3 (civil court and trade-union agreement), 2017 Q5(Or)(b) (ferry accident / notional extension), 2018 Q4 (legal versus justified strike).
- Case-law demands are explicit: 2018 Q4 ("recent case law"), 2023 Q5 ("relevant case law"), 2024 Q5(Or) and 2025 Q5(Or) ("leading case laws"), 2025 Q6(Or) ("judicial decisions"), 2023 Q5(Or) (*Bandhua Mukti Morcha*).
- Exact repetition is tolerated: the retrenchment statement appeared with near-identical wording in 2015 Q4(Or), 2017 Q4, 2019 Q4(Or), 2020 Q5 and 2023 Q4(Or). A 2026 reappearance is more likely to be a rephrase than a fresh topic.

# 13. 2026 Prediction Methodology

1. **Dataset construction:** 10 verified papers; 2022 excluded; 2020 excluded from structure statistics but included in topic counts.
2. **Mapping:** all 69 numbered questions assigned to official unit/topic IDs; multi-topic questions mapped to a primary topic with secondary links recorded.
3. **Repetition typing:** exact [ER], rephrased [R], same-provision/different-question [S], broad-topic recurrence [B] kept separate so a single question is not inflated into several independent appearances.
4. **Counting:** each paper counts at most once per topic for a given slot type; objective subparts are counted as individual objective appearances but never as independent long-answer occurrences.
5. **Confidence factors:** (a) recurrence count, (b) recency, (c) reappearance after a gap, (d) exact versus rephrased history, (e) marks and format history, (f) slot stability, (g) syllabus centrality, (h) underrepresentation only where the syllabus makes the topic central.
6. **Priority factors:** syllabus centrality, consequences of not preparing, and whether the topic can appear inside a compound question — deliberately independent of confidence.
7. **Prediction wording:** written as examination-style questions derived strictly from official topic wording; alternative formulations stay inside the same official topic.
8. **Model paper:** assembled from the nine-paper standard architecture, selecting one main and one `Or` for each long slot from the ranked predictions, with marks totalling 80 and clearly labelled unofficial.
9. **Verification:** every claimed historical reference was checked against the archive; every prediction checked against the official topic list.

# 14. Prediction Rating Definitions

**Prediction confidence** — strength of the historical evidence only (not probability):

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Very High** | Topic appeared in 6+ of 10 papers, often in the same slot, with recent and/or verbatim recurrence |
| **High** | Appeared in 4–5 papers, or in every recent paper in a recognisable form |
| **Moderate** | Appeared in 2–3 papers, or only as objectives/notes, or after a long gap |
| **Low** | One verified appearance, or none as a standalone question |

**Revision priority** — independent preparation importance:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Syllabus-central, repeatedly examined, and a miss would cost a full long-answer slot |
| **High** | Core statutory area with strong or recurrent examination presence |
| **Medium** | Examinable and syllabus-prescribed; often embedded in compound questions |
| **General** | Lower historical yield but required for full-syllabus protection |

**Explicit separation:** e.g. U3-T05 (annual leave with wages) has **Low** prediction confidence (one appearance, 2016 Q5(Or)) but **Medium** revision priority because it is an official topic and can be paired with a Factories Act question. Conversely U5-T01 has **Very High** confidence and **High** priority.

No percentage, probability or statistical forecasting model is used. Ratings are qualitative judgments grounded in the cited evidence.

## D. Final revision checklist
See Section 22. Use it topic by topic, not prediction by prediction.

## E. Answer-writing guidance
- **Definitions and short notes:** give the statutory definition first, the Act and (only if verified) the section, then one line on legal effect. Two-mark answers need no introduction.
- **Statutory questions:** structure as (i) object/definition, (ii) statutory provisions in the syllabus's own sequence, (iii) authorities or consequences. Use the syllabus wording for headings; do not invent section numbers.
- **Long explanatory questions:** follow the marks split printed in the question (`4+8`, `6+6`, `2+5+5`). Each component gets its own heading. Conclude briefly.
- **Critical/analytical questions:** state the proposition, explain the conflicting considerations (legality versus justification; prevention versus compensation), support with the case named in the paper or syllabus, and give a reasoned conclusion.
- **Distinction questions:** use a two-column or point-by-point format — basis, first concept, second concept, one-line conclusion. Never merge subquestions.
- **Historical wording:** when quoting an older paper, preserve its Act names and section numbers as printed; add a separate note if the current legal position differs.
- **Time discipline:** with 80 marks in three hours, the 10 objective items and 10 short-note marks should take roughly 25–30 minutes together, leaving about two hours for the five 12-mark questions.
